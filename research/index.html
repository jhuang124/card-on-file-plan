01 · Summary

Summary

The card-on-file plan solves the smallest part of reactivation: typing a card. The services that win back the most subscribers treat reactivation as an account state with its own entry points, its own screen, and its own offer rules, and they spend most of their effort on subscribers who never meant to leave.

Where churn comes from
~1 in 3 lost subscribers never chose to leave
Payment failure is 35 to 38% of digital-media churn (Recurly network, INMA). Our plan hides expired cards instead of fixing them.
The dominant pattern
Sign in, then one state-aware button
Netflix, Strava, Disney+, Google Play and YouTube all branch the button on account state. No one we could verify charges a stored card silently.
What offers do
88% higher conversion with 20% off
Piano's A/B/C test on churned readers: 0.38% at full price, 0.71% with a discount. The plan has no offer logic.
What returns are worth
30% of streaming gross adds are returners
Antenna, 2023 premium SVOD. 41% of cancels came back within 12 months. We have no equivalent number for The Atlantic (TK).

Five Findings That Change the Approach

  1. Reactivation starts before the lapse. Apple recovers 75% of billing failures within 16 days when a grace period is on; publishers historically retried 2.6 times against 13 for broadcasters. Our card-on-file plan does nothing for a subscriber whose card failed, which is the largest single reactivation audience.
  2. The best flows are state-aware, not checkout-aware. Netflix tells lapsed members to use Sign In (not Get Started) and shows a restart-only screen. Strava's "Resubscribe Now" reactivates a cancelled-but-not-expired plan with no charge. Google Play's "Resubscribe" undoes a pending cancel on the same purchase. The verb changes with the state; the plans page is never the first stop.
  3. A true silent one-click on a stored card is not an industry standard. Across 17 consumer services, the closest patterns are pre-expiry restarts and platform-held payment (Apple, Google, Microsoft). Where the member has fully lapsed, every verified flow shows the plan, the price and a consent action first. Federal law (ROSCA) and California's renewal law require that anyway.
  4. Publishers are behind the rest of the market. No major news publisher we checked documents a self-serve restart; NYT, WSJ and WaPo route returners through standard offer pages, chat or phone. That is the gap The Atlantic can close, and a reason not to benchmark only against publishers.
  5. Offers need rules, not blanket discounts. Apple gates win-back offers on months subscribed and months since lapse, with a cooldown between offers. Spotify date-gates its re-engagement offer. Apple's guidance: wait before discounting, or you train subscribers to cancel for the deal.

03 · Bluesky

The Experience Space

Reactivation is four moments, not one screen. The ideas below are organized by moment, with the competitor evidence for each, so the question becomes which moments to invest in rather than which checkout to build.

Moment 1 · Before the Lapse: Keep Them

Payment-failure recovery with access kept

YouTube: 3 days to fix, then 30 days paused with retries · Apple grace period: 3, 16 or 28 days · Netflix "on hold"

A dedicated state with its own copy ("There's a problem with your last payment") and a one-field fix, shown in product, by email and by push. Access continues during the grace window.

Ours: not designed. Zuora retries exist; the subscriber-facing state does not.

Automatic card updating

Stripe Card Account Updater is on for cards stored on a Customer; Zuora offers Stripe real-time updater as a tenant feature

Replaced and reissued cards update themselves, so the renewal never fails. Invisible to the subscriber, which is the point.

Ours: status in our Zuora tenant is TK. Cheapest lever on the list if it is off.

Pause instead of cancel

Audible: 30, 60 or 90 days, "Keep my membership" to resume · Amazon Prime: 1 month auto-resume or up to a year · YouTube: 1 to 6 months

A paused subscriber is not a lapsed one. Resume is one click because nothing was torn down.

Ours: not offered. Zuora supports suspend and resume natively.

Expiry warnings before renewal

Standard dunning practice (Recurly, Paddle Retain: up to 4 emails)

"Your Visa ending 4242 expires next month" with a one-field update, sent before the renewal attempt rather than after it fails.

Ours: TK whether any pre-expiry notice is sent.

Moment 2 · At the Lapse: Make Coming Back Easy to Imagine

State-aware restart on the account page

Netflix "Your membership has already been canceled" banner + "Restart membership" · Strava "Resubscribe Now" · Disney+ "Restart Subscription" then "Agree"

Before the term ends, one button undoes the cancel with no charge and the same billing date. After it ends, the button becomes a restart with a price.

Ours: the lapsed account page links to the plans page. There is no undo-cancel state at all.

Tell them what they keep

Netflix keeps profiles, My List and payment "as if you never canceled" · Audible restores locked titles on rejoin · Strava "no data is lost"

The cancellation confirmation and the lapsed state both say what is preserved and for how long. It lowers the cost of returning before anyone asks them to.

Ours: the v4 prototype's "everything you had is back" copy is the right idea; nothing says it at cancel time.

Moment 3 · Coming Back: Recognize Them Wherever They Appear

Sign in first, not sign up first

Netflix's help page tells lapsed members to choose "Sign In", never "Get Started" or "Join Now"

The acquisition funnel is the wrong place for a returner. The sign-in path should detect the lapsed state and route to restart, and the subscribe page should offer "Already subscribed before? Sign in" above the plans.

Ours: every entry lands on plans. The paywall concept assumes the reader is already signed in.

Email that deep-links into the restart

Netflix "Restart to watch now" on a content card · Apple win-back redemption URLs · Spotify "Rejoin Premium" (with reported eligibility errors)

Content leads, the button restarts. The link carries identity (a signed token or magic link) so a signed-out reader lands on the restart page, not on sign-in.

Ours: the email concept exists; it assumes an active session.

A restart page that is not checkout

HBO Max "Welcome Back" screen · Netflix welcome-back flow · Recurly's "Richard, we want you back" prompt with "Resume Subscription"

Previous plan named, price and renewal terms stated, card on file shown, one consent button. Edits available but collapsed.

Ours: this is the one-click restart concept. It is the strongest thing in the prototype branch and it is not in the plan.

Recognized paywall for lapsed readers

Piano's onsite winback modal on the second pageview (0.38% to 0.71% conversion) · Recurly homepage prompt for churned segments

The gate a lapsed reader hits should not be the gate a stranger hits: it names them, names the plan, and restarts in place.

Ours: prototyped for signed-in readers only. The signed-out case needs email capture and a magic link.

Moment 4 · After the Return: Make It Stick and Make It Worth It

Rule-gated win-back offers

Apple: minimum paid duration, 1 to 24 months since lapse, cooldown between offers · Spotify: 3 months at a set price for past subscribers · Piano: test 10 to 30%, never below new-business pricing

Who gets an offer, when, and how often is a policy, not a coupon. Apple's advice is to wait before discounting and deepen the offer as time since churn grows.

Ours: no offer logic. Lapsed readers see new-subscriber pricing or full price.

Offer the right plan, not the old one

Apple targets downgraders in-app · Piano recommends emotional versus transactional framing tests

A reader who left over price should be offered Digital, or annual instead of monthly, before being asked to restart Print & Digital at full price.

Ours: the restart concept preselects the previous plan. Good default, no alternative.

Continuity after payment

ScreensDesign pattern: return the user to the content that prompted the return, confirm the plan and the management route

From the paywall, restart unlocks the article in place. From email, it opens the story. The confirmation says what was charged and when it renews.

Ours: prototyped in both restart layouts ("Continue reading", "You're back").

Decline is a valid outcome

ScreensDesign pattern: offer the free experience or an exit; do not repeat the prompt on every visit · Piano caps exposure at about 2 per 30 days

A returner who says no today should not meet the same modal tomorrow. Frequency caps and a real "not now" are part of the design.

Ours: "Not now, continue reading" exists in the gate concept; no cap logic.
04 · Competitors

Competitor Clusters

Seventeen consumer services and the major publishers sort into six approaches. Most publishers sit in the weakest one. The strongest services combine three: a state-aware restart, payment-failure recovery, and rule-gated offers.

Cluster A · The most common premium pattern

Sign In, Then One State-Aware Button

Netflix · Strava · Disney+ · Google Play · YouTube Premium · NOW (UK)

The member signs in and sees their real account state with a single action matched to it. Before the term ends it is an undo ("Undo Cancel Request", "Resubscribe Now", "Restart Subscription" then "Agree") that reactivates the existing subscription with no charge and the same billing date. After the term ends it becomes a restart that states the price. Payment and preferences are kept "as if you never canceled."

Sign In→Account state banner→Restart membership→Watching
  • Netflix keeps profiles, My List, game saves and payment info; the billing date stays the same unless the welcome-back flow runs. help.netflix.com
  • Strava: "Resubscribe Now" on the Account page "will only reactivate your existing subscription; you won't be charged." support.strava.com
  • Google Play's "Resubscribe" undoes a pending cancel on the same purchase token; after expiry it is a new purchase and the button may be hidden. developer.android.com
  • NOW splits the copy on the payment date: "Restart membership" before it, "Add memberships" after. nowtv.com
Fit for The Atlantic: highNeeds: an undo-cancel state in Zuora
Cluster B · The common default

Welcome Back, Then the Normal Funnel

HBO Max · Audible · Amazon Prime · Spotify · Patreon

The service recognizes the returner with a welcome-back screen, then sends them through the standard plan picker and payment form. History and preferences carry over; payment often does not. HBO Max: "Choose a subscription plan and billing period", then "Add your payment method." Audible: pick a plan, "Sign up", follow the prompts. Amazon: "Rejoin Amazon Prime", "Activate your Prime Membership", add a payment method.

  • This is what The Atlantic's plan builds, with one improvement: the card is preselected. help.hbomax.com · help.audible.com
  • Spotify's legacy plans and zero-priced trials "can't be reactivated", so those users get a fresh plan picker. support.spotify.com
Fit: this is where the plan landsAdequate, not differentiating
Cluster C · Where most publishers are

Full Re-Acquisition, No Recognition

The New York Times · The Wall Street Journal · The Washington Post · The Guardian app · The Atlantic today

No documented self-serve restart. Returners hit the standard offer page, or call and chat. NYT's help center covers cancellation by account, phone and chat and nothing on resubscribing; WSJ routes through its Customer Center and phone; WaPo's documented path is a save offer at cancel time, not a return path. The Guardian's app shows a lapsed reader "You don't currently have a subscription under this email address" with "Support us now."

  • Returning NYT readers are reportedly offered the standard $1 introductory rate, the same as a stranger. Poynter, 2021
  • Only the Las Vegas Review-Journal, BNP Media and a blox digital beta publish any win-back mechanics, all email-led. Mather · Omeda
Fit: the baseline to leaveOpportunity: no publisher owns this
Cluster D · The platform does it

Platform-Held Payment With Rule-Gated Offers

Apple App Store · Google Play · Xbox / Microsoft

The stored payment method belongs to the platform, so "Renew" or "Resubscribe" in system settings is as close to one-tap as the market gets. Apple's win-back offers (September 2024) surface in Settings, in-app, on the product page and via a shareable link, gated by minimum paid duration, 1 to 24 months since lapse, and a cooldown. Google Play's win-back offers on the Play Store entered early access in September 2026.

  • Apple's example offer: $9.99 for six months against $39.99 a year. developer.apple.com
  • Apple recovers 40% of billing issues within 3 days and 90% within 28 when grace periods are on; about 80M lapsed subscriptions recovered in a year. Apple Tech Talk
Fit: free for app-store subscribersNeeds: offers configured in App Store Connect (TK)
Cluster E · Prevent the lapse

Recovery States, Grace Periods and Pause

YouTube Premium · Netflix · Audible · Apple · Google Play · Amazon Prime

Payment failure gets its own named state and screen rather than a quiet cancellation. YouTube gives 3 days, then pauses for 30 with continued retries and an "Update payment method" warning. Netflix puts the account "on hold because of a problem with your last payment." Audible retries every 4 days up to 6 times. Pause is offered as the alternative to cancel, with a defined resume.

Charge fails→Grace: access kept, retries→Hold: "Update payment"→Cancel
  • Google Play's optimized recovery (grace + hold + in-app messaging) recovers about 30 to 35% versus 10 to 15% with defaults. RevenueCat
  • Stripe Smart Retries default to 8 attempts in 2 weeks and can pause instead of cancel. docs.stripe.com
Fit: highest leverage per dollarNeeds: Zuora retry and updater settings confirmed (TK)
Cluster F · Pull them back

Onsite and Email Win-Back With an Offer

Piano (publisher tooling) · Recurly Engage · Netflix · Spotify · Omeda / BNP Media

A churned-reader segment gets a prompt where they already are: the second pageview, the homepage, the inbox. Recurly's "1-click resubscribe" fires a "Richard, we want you back" prompt with "Resume Subscription" and requires card data on file, with an optional double opt-in. Piano's A/B/C test is the only publisher-grade number: 0.38% conversion at full price, 0.71% at 20% off, against a 0.19% median.

  • BNP Media: discounts of 10 to 30% growing with time away; 1,500+ lapsed subscribers reactivated. Omeda
  • Spotify's re-engagement offer is activated "by submitting payment details and paying the advertised price": an offer, not a one-click. spotify.com
Fit: high, once a restart page exists to land on

07 · Constraints

Constraints That Shape the Design

Consent is required, so "silent" one-click is out

ROSCA requires clear disclosure of material terms, express informed consent before the charge, and a simple cancel. California's renewal law (effective July 2025) adds express affirmative consent, retained proof of consent, and cancel-by-the-same-channel. Stripe's card-network rules require explicit consent for the purpose each time a saved card is charged for a new agreement. A restart is a new contract: show plan, price, renewal cadence and cancel terms, then one consent click, logged. The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated in July 2025; ROSCA enforcement continues (Amazon settled for $2.5B in September 2025). 15 USC 8403 · California ARL summary · Stripe CIT/MIT

Accidental restarts need an undo, not a second click

Nielsen Norman Group's guidance on one-click purchase risk: confirm immediately and offer cancellation on the spot rather than adding a confirmation dialog people click through. The two-day refund window in today's auto-renew copy is the right mechanism; the confirmation screen should say it. nngroup.com

Stripe's own checkout will not show our saved cards

Cards saved during a subscription get allow_redisplay: limited, which Stripe Checkout does not prefill; Stripe's recommended returning-customer route is Link. This confirms the plan's decision to show Zuora's default rather than Stripe's list, and it means any restart page is custom UI over our own purchase call. support.stripe.com

A restart is a new Zuora subscription, not a revival

Zuora documents resume for suspended subscriptions and nothing for reactivating a cancelled one; Stripe and Recurly say the same about their products. The undo-cancel pattern (Cluster A) therefore needs cancel-at-term-end to leave the subscription alive until the date, which is a question for our Zuora setup (TK). Pause maps to Zuora suspend and resume natively. docs.zuora.com

08 · Recommendations

Recommendations

Keep the backend from the card-on-file plan; it is the right foundation. Change the customer-facing bet from "an easier payment step" to "a reactivation state with entry points", and start with the subscribers who never meant to leave.

1

Fix Involuntary Churn Before Building Any Restart UI

It is the largest reactivation audience, it needs no new customer journey to start, and the industry numbers are the strongest in this review. Our plan hides expired cards, which is the opposite of recovery.

Do: confirm Zuora retry settings and whether Stripe's account updater is enabled for our tenant; add a pre-expiry card notice; design a "problem with your payment" state in the app and on the account page with a one-field fix and a grace window; in the restart page, show the expired card with an inline editor instead of hiding it (the stacked prototype already does this).
Leverage: highestMostly configuration plus one screen
2

Make Restart an Account State, Not a Checkout Variant

Every strong competitor branches one button on the subscriber's state. Our returners still walk Account → Plans → Checkout, and the subscribe page cannot tell a returner from a stranger.

Do: promote the one-click restart concept into the plan as the primary lapsed experience: "Welcome back, Jane", previous plan named, card and address on file, price and renewal terms stated, one consent button, edits collapsed. Add "Already subscribed before? Sign in" above the plans. Add an undo-cancel state for subscribers still in term if Zuora's cancel-at-term-end supports it. Keep card-preselected checkout as the fallback.
Leverage: highNeeds the Figma comps to match
3

Build Entry Points That Carry Identity

Sessions end after 24 hours, so most lapsed readers who return are signed out and no recognized gate or restart page can reach them. Netflix and Apple solve this with links that carry the member into the restart.

Do: win-back emails with signed links that land on the restart page (sign-in only if the token has expired); a lapsed-reader variant of the paywall for signed-in readers; for signed-out readers at the paywall, an email field that sends the same signed link. Push notifications in the app for lapsed app subscribers.
Leverage: highSecurity review for signed links
4

Write Offer Rules, Then Test Them

Discounting lifted Piano's win-back conversion 88% and revenue 78%, and Apple's guidance is that timing and tenure rules prevent training readers to cancel for a deal. We have no rules at all.

Do: define eligibility by tenure and months since lapse, a cooldown between offers, and an exposure cap (about 2 prompts per 30 days). Test 10 to 30% off against full price and against a plan switch (Digital, or annual instead of monthly). Never price below new-subscriber offers. Track retention at 30, 60, 90 and 180 days, not just conversion.
Leverage: high for voluntary churn
5

Offer Pause and Say What They Keep

A paused subscriber keeps their card, their plan and their habit; a lapsed one loses all three. Audible, Amazon and YouTube make resume a true one-click because nothing was torn down.

Do: add pause (30, 60, 90 days) to the cancel flow, mapped to Zuora suspend and resume; at cancel and on the lapsed account page, state what is kept (saved stories, newsletters, card on file) and for how long.
Leverage: medium; depends on cancel-reason mix
6

Configure App-Store Win-Back Offers Now

For app-store subscribers the platform already holds the payment method, handles eligibility and surfaces the offer in Settings and on the product page. It costs configuration, not engineering.

Do: set up Apple win-back offers in App Store Connect with the same tenure and lapse rules as Recommendation 4; enable the billing grace period; watch Google Play's win-back offers as they leave early access.
Leverage: medium, near zero costCurrent app-store setup: TK

09 · Before Committing

Open Questions

Four internal numbers decide whether the card-on-file plan as written targets the larger or the smaller opportunity.

  1. Lapse reason mix. Of subscribers who lapsed in the last 12 months, what share canceled on purpose versus failed payment versus trial or gift ending? (Recommendation 1 versus 2 and 4.)
  2. Eligibility waterfall. Of lapsed subscribers, how many have a Zuora account, a card default, a card that is still valid, and were signed in on their first return visit? Each condition removes a share of the card-on-file audience.
  3. Return entry points. Where do reactivations start today: paywall, email, account page, app, or support? How many of those readers were signed in?
  4. Payment-step abandonment. Of returning customers who reach checkout, how many abandon at the payment step versus earlier? If the answer is "earlier", card on file is not the bottleneck.

Also TK: whether Stripe's account updater is enabled in our Zuora tenant; whether cancel-at-term-end keeps a Zuora subscription alive until the date; the Atlantic's current App Store grace-period and offer configuration; and whether any pre-expiry card notice is sent today.
